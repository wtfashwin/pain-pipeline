Test manual invoice follow-up first.

A narrow opportunity for freelancers and small agencies who keep existing invoices and need a clear next action. This is the strongest hypothesis in this shortlist. It has no verified product demand or paying users.

Overview

Context: Compare three everyday admin problems for an eight-hour MVP. Seller baseline: a proposed tool; no named account, active deal, direct interviews, or validated customer outcomes. Pipeline infrastructure cannot establish willingness to pay.

First hypothesis

Overdue invoice follow-up

Documented business cost and established paid products. Test a manual queue that preserves the existing invoicing workflow.

Backup

CSV import diagnostics

Concrete user reports, but no representative prevalence or standalone utility demand. Choose one destination/schema before building.

Defer

Job follow-up tracker

Unlimited job tracking is already free at Teal. Paid career bundles do not establish tracker-only willingness to pay.

Frequency and cost: bounded evidence

The UK government study estimates 28% of UK businesses affected by late payment, 22% spending staff time pursuing unpaid invoices, and 86 hours a year per affected business. Its 1,455-response survey ran in January–February 2025 and asked about the preceding year. These figures describe UK businesses, not most people or global freelancers.

A February 2026 Invoice Ninja discussion reports reminder configuration that produced no apparent send or log evidence. Grist #2556 describes repeated waits during CSV import; #1162 describes blank matching fields producing duplicate records, reproduced by a maintainer in 2024. Reports are anecdotes, not verified current product defects, demand records, or permission to contact authors.

No evidence here establishes a problem faced by most people. No reliable independent first-person job follow-up timing complaint was established in the bounded search. Full source content is not retained in this brief.

Comparison takeaways

Invoice workflow: pressure from Invoice Ninja and Chaser. A proposed manual queue must save work without asking users to move their invoices. CSV: mixed. Pain exists, but paid products address a broader developer task. Jobs: price evidence not determined. Free tracking weakens the standalone proposition.

No unmet competitor gap is verified. Human control, explicit next actions, and keeping existing invoices are positioning hypotheses. Privacy, security, and reminder availability are not established competitor weaknesses.

Guidance

Five real demand records before the build

  1. Interview five freelancers or small agency operators who personally manage invoice follow-ups. No outreach is authorized by this document.
  2. Record a recent redacted example, existing workflow, time spent, consequence, current software, evidence reference, and any negative response. Avoid client identities, bank data, and unnecessary invoice documents.
  3. Walk through a paper/local queue using their example. Distinguish forgotten admin from disputes or a customer's inability to pay.
  4. Test US$5/month as a proposed price, or a local-currency offer explicitly fixed before interviews. Record interest in a two-week priced pilot; an answer is not a payment. Charging requires separate authority.
  5. Require five real records and human selection. Proposed go rule: at least three recurring admin-pain accounts and two explicit priced-pilot commitments. These are decision thresholds, not market benchmarks. Verify actual use afterwards.

Kill or revise if thresholds fail, existing tools solve the problem, duplicate entry costs more than it saves, or useful value requires automatic sending, accounting sync, payments, legal escalation, or multi-user access. Five interviews cannot estimate prevalence.

Seven observable MVP features

Bounded CSV importValid fixture imports; duplicate IDs, missing fields, invalid dates, and oversize files yield row errors and no partial commit.
Manual entry/editA saved invoice and edited due date survive reload; invalid amounts cannot be saved.
Overdue queueExplicit-date test includes unpaid past-due invoices and excludes due-today, future, paid, and archived items.
Next action/historyA follow-up date and timestamped action persist; history never implies that a message was sent.
Reviewed draftExplicit click creates an editable reminder with correct details; copy records only a copy, with no network send.
Settled/disputed controlsSettled items leave the queue; disputed items require human review and get no automatic escalation.
Export/clearFixture round-trip preserves values; confirmed clear persists after reload; diagnostic logs contain no invoice content or secrets.

Estimated eight-hour build: 1 hour schema/red tests, 2 import/storage, 2 queue/actions/drafts, 1 export/clear, 2 accessibility/regression/local demo. Write tests first and inspect the red failure cause. Stop after three unresolved repair attempts, retain failures, and report honestly. Use synthetic development fixtures.

Cuts: no model calls, scraping, automatic sends, payments, interest calculation, tax/legal advice, accounting integrations, or production deployment. Generated shell text must never be executed. Reviewed local argv is not a sandbox. Network sends, public posting, spending, and deployment need explicit authority.

Response strategy

Say: decide which invoice needs a manual follow-up and retain its next action. Clarify: what is painful enough to pay for, and why existing tools fail. Prove: correct queue behavior and observed review time. Do not say: guaranteed faster payment, recovered revenue, certified security, universal demand, or measured savings.
